# <span style="color:#fabd2f">Hormonal regulation
## <span style="color:#fabd2f">Hormones
- Produced by `endocrine glands`
- Transported in `blood`
- Regulate `far away organs/behaviour`
  - Temperament/personal behaviour is influenced
- Different `receptors` on different organs can `pick up on specific hormones`
- High level of hormones in blood: `Hyperproduction`
- Low level/no hormone in blood : `Hypoproduction`[^1]
- 2 types:
  - Peptides/proteins
  - Steroids
[^1]: From greek 'hypo', meaning 'low'
### <span style="color:#fabd2f">Peptides
- Polar molecules --> Can't enter cell
- They have to have a secondary messenger molecule inside the cell
### <span style="color:#fabd2f"> Steroids
- `Non-polar, fat soluble` molecules --> Can move through cell membrane
- Can enter `cell or nucleus`
- Receptor `inside cytoplasm`
- Can influence `DNA production directly`

## <span style="color:#fabd2f"> Regulatory feedback loop
- Important role in maintaining homeostasis
- Pituitary produces tropic hormone --> Signals release of other hormones
- Hyperproduction of a hormone --> `Inhibition of future production` of that hormone

## <span style="color:#fabd2f">Hormonal vs nervous system
- Nervous system is `faster`
- Hormonal system causes `longer term changes`


## <span style="color:#fabd2f">Glands
- Important endocrine glands:
  - Pineal --> Produces melatonin
  - Pituitary
  - Thyroid/parathyroid
  - Pancreas
  - Adrenal
  - Ovaries/testes
### <span style="color:#fabd2f"> Thyroid
- Produces `thyroxine`:
  - `Metabolic regulation`
  - Regulates metabolic rate of cells
  - Hyperthyroidism:
    - Nervousness
    - high appetite
    - weight loss
    - goiter
  - Hypothyroidism:
    - Causes dwarfism with low IQ
    - In adults it causes sluggishness, mixoedema, goiter
  - Causes of hypothyroidism:
    - Lack of iodine
- Produces `calcitonine`:
  - Removes calcium from blood
  - Moves it into bones/urine
### <span style="color:#fabd2f"> Parathyroid glands
- Four small globes `behind the thyroid`
- Increases calcium levels of blood by taking it from bones
- Increases intestinal calcium absorption
- Requires Vitamin D

###<span style="color:#fabd2f"> Pancreas
- Both exocrine and endocrine gland
- Exocrine part produces `pancreatic juice`
  - Contains all kinds of enzymes to break down different nutrients
- Endocrine part:
  - $\alpha$ cells produce `glucagon`
  - $\beta$ cells produce `insulin`, which signals cells to take up glucose

###<span style="color:#fabd2f"> Adrenal glands
- two of them
- Parts:
  - Medulla
  - Cortex
- Cortex:
  - Produces Glucocorticoids
- Medulla:
  - Produces epinephrine

###<span style="color:#fabd2f"> Pituitary gland
- Close contact with the `hypothalamus`
- Posterior lobe --> Only `stores hormones` (Antidiuretic Hormone, Oxytocin)
- Oxytocin --> Responsible for pro-social behaviour
- Produces many hormones, inlcuding GH (Growth horomone)
- Growth hormone:
  - Increases blood sugar
  - affects metabolism
- Anterior lobe influences:
  - Parathyroid
  - Pancreas
  - Adrenal medulla
  - Outermost layer of the adrenal cortex
