# <span style="color:#fabd2f"> Intro



### <span style="color:#fabd2f"> Why is economics a social science?
- `Social` because it is concerned with understanding the way actors[^1] react to `changes in certain factors`
- `Science` because it is systematic with its own approaches and methods to calculation and measurement --> impersonal
[^1]: e.g: households, businesses, industries
## <span style="color:#fabd2f"> Microeconomics v. Macroeconomics

- Key difference: The level at which we examine actors (individual <--> collective)


### <span style="color:#fabd2f">Microeconomics
- Concerned with `understanding behaviour of individual actors` (households, firms, industries)
- E.g: "How willing is a given household to consume at a given moment with given factors?"

### <span style="color:#fabd2f">Macroeconomics
- Concerned with understanding the behaviour of a given market as a whole:
- Measures economic metrics:
  - Inflation
  - Unemployment
  - GDP

### <span style="color:#fabd2f">Global economics
- Economies cannot function alone without `trade`
- Global economics seeks to examine the transactions, connections between the markets/economies of countries

## <span style="color:#fabd2f">9 Core economic concepts

### <span style="color:#fabd2f">Scarcity
- The limited availability 
- Humans have `unlimited needs` <--> `Limited resources`
- Basic issue of economics
  - "How can we use limited resources to satisfy the maximum level of unlimited needs & wants?"

### <span style="color:#fabd2f">Choice
- Impossible to fulfill all human needs  -
  - We must make `choices` about sacrificing one thing so that we can have another
- `Opportunity cost`:
  - The thing that has to be given up when making a choice

### <span style="color:#fabd2f">Efficiency
- The extent to which resources are used to achieve maximum possible output or benefit
- Can be used to justify government intervention in the case of low market efficiency
- Typically used with `micro-economics`

### <span style="color:#fabd2f"> Equity
- Resources are not equally distributed by default
- Fairness in the distribution of income, wealth, resources, opportunities
- Mostly used with `macro-economics`

### <span style="color:#fabd2f">Well-being
- Broader concept
- Material and non-material quality of life of an individual/society
- Examines certain `subjective factors` that impact the happiness of the population
  - Access to medical care
  - Access to quality education
  - Freedoms (of speech, religion, choice, etc.)
  - Opportunity distribution (rural v. urban)
- Used in `global economics`

### <span style="color:#fabd2f">Sustainability
- Connected with `scarcity`
- Ability of current economic activity to meet demands without compromising future generations
  - Future generations will also have needs & wants --> They will also need some of the limited resources
- "How do we use scarce resources to ensure future generations also have access to them?"


### <span style="color:#fabd2f">Change
- Changes in economic conditions/behaviour/sturctures over time
- Has `cause` and an `implication(s)`
- We examine both the causes and effects of change


### <span style="color:#fabd2f">Interdependence
- Typically used in `global economics`
- The idea that one actor's behaviour will affect the others
- e.g A country moving from a primary sector to a secondary will change its imports/exports --> This will affect the other countries it trades with

### <span style="color:#fabd2f"> Intervention
- Used to mean `government intervention`
- Government action to influence `economic outcomes`
- "Is intervention necessary?"
- "What degree of intervention is necessary?"

## Identify the concept
1. Egypt has limited freshwater resources.
   - `Scarcity`
2. Egyptian government must decide where to allocate water
   - `Choice`
3. Farmer might give up growing a water-intensive crop
   - `Choice`
4. Government invests in irrigation systems
   - `Efficiency`
5. The government decides that low income households should also have affordable access to water
   - `Equity`
6. Water shortages may reduce quality of life
   - `Well-being`
7. Excessive groundwater extraction might make water access harder for future generations
   - `Sustainability`
8. Economic/Population growth and climate change might change the demand for water
   - `Change`
9. Egypt's water situation is affected by developments/policies of other countries along the Nile
   - `Interdependence`
10. The government introduces restrictions on water consumption
    - `Intervention`
