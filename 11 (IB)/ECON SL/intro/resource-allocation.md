# <span style="color:#fabd2f">Resource allocation
- Deals with the following questions:
    - WHAT to produce (what kind of production should resource be allocated to?)
    - HOW to produce (what is the best way to allocate resources?)
      - `Technically efficient` production:
        - `Using less` scarce resources than other methods
      - Economically efficient production:
        - `Making cheaper` products
    -  Distribution --> FOR WHOM to produce
       - Who can get our produced goods?
- Different economic systems --> Different answers to this questions

## <span style="color:#fabd2f">Economic systems and allocation

### <span style="color:#fabd2f">Pure market economy
- Characterization:
  - `No intervention` by the government
  - `Market mechanism` operates freely (supply/demand are unrestricted)
  - Unlimited `competition` --> Forces innovation, incentivizes low prices[^1]
  - `Private ownership` of production factors
  - `Quick response` to market changes

- Effects:
  - Maximizes `economic efficiency`


[^1]: Creates extremely inequal income distribution, environmental damage

### <span style="color:#fabd2f">Pure planned economy
- Characterization:
  - Very `low competition`
  - Government has a `significant role`
  - Government:
    - Allocates all resources
    - Dictates production goals
  - Prone to `corruption`
  - `Collective ownership` of production factors
- Effects:
  - Government is bad at predicting market demands --> Excess demands --> `Shortage`
  - Shortages can give rise to `black markets`

## <span style="color:#fabd2f">How can we divide a bar of chocolate among ten students?
- We can divide the chocolate ten ways evenly
- The best economics student gets the bar
- The older a student is the more chocolate they get
- Taller students get more chocolate
- Men don't get any chocolate
- The hungriest students get more chocolate
- Students who agree i'm the best economics student get more chocolate
- Competition-based division

## Arguments against pure market capitalism
- Perfect competition does not and cannot exist without government intervention
- Social gap would be widened by the cutthroat competition
- High environmental damage / Bad quality
- Bank failure
- Lack of competition means companies can focus on creating the highest quality goods
- No burden of choice

- We still believe that:
  - Pure planned economies can work
  - A pure market capitalist system works to widen social gaps
  - A pure market capitalist system has no welfare or social safety net (e.g firefighters, police, ambulances)
  - Pure market economies are dangerously prone to monopolies without government oversight
