# <span style="color:#fabd2f">Price-elasticity of supply
- Measures the responsiveness of the `quantity supplied` to `changes in price`
    > PES = |ΔQ<sub>s</sub>/ΔP|
    - ΔQ<sub>s</sub> --> % Change in quantity supplied
    - ΔP --> % Change in unit price
- Can be thought of as the `slope of the supply curve`
- `Always positive`
- Larger responsiveness: `Elastic`
- Smaller responsiveness: `Inelastic`

|PES|x|Explanation|
|-|-|-|
|0|Perfectly inelastic|Quantity supplied `never changes`, no matter the price change|
||||
|0-1|Inelastic|Quantity supplied `changes less` than the price|
|1|Unit-elastic|Quantity supplied changes `as much as` the price|
|>1|Elastic|Quantity supplied `changes more` than the price|
||||
|∞|Perfectly elastic|Quantity supplied changes with essentially no change in the price|

## <span style="color:#fabd2f">Determinants of PES
- `Time period:`
  - Short: No time to change Q<sub>s</sub> immediately --> Less elasticity
  - Long: Enough time for producers to adjust --> More elastic
- `Mobility of factors of production:`
  - Sometimes extra factors of production must be acquired to increase production
  - E.g: Production which requires unskilled labour is more elastic, Production that requires specialist labour is less elastic
- `Spare capacity of firms:`
  - Unused production capacity of factories/production plants
  - More spare capacity means easier production increase --> More elasticity
  - Less spare capacity means harder to increase production --> Less elasticity
- `Storage capacity:`
  - Bigger stockpile --> Easier to release more stock --> More elasticity
  - Smaller stockpile --> Harder to release extra stock --> Less elasticity
- `Rate of cost increase:`
  - High production cost increase --> Hard to expand production --> Inelastic
  - Small production cost increase --> Easier to expand production --> Elastic
