# <span style="color:#fabd2f">Indirect tax

## <span style="color:#fabd2f">Definition
- A tax imposed on `spending`
- Consumer --> Producer --> Government
- Types:
  - Excise tax (tobacco, gas, alcohol)
  - Sales tax (VAT)

## Reasons for imposing indirect taxes
- Source of `government revenue`
- Discourage consumption of `demerit goods`
- Redistribute income
- Improve resource allocation --> Encourage/discourage production

## Types of indirect tax
### Specific tax
- A fixed amount of tax per unit
- Tax paid `doesn't change with price`

### Ad valorem / percentage tax
- The tax is a percentage of the price of the product (e.g VAT)
- Higher price --> Higher tax


## Effects on Marshall cross
- Indirect tax --> Higher cost --> `Suply curve shifts left`
- Higher consumer price
- Lower producer price
### Changes in price:
  - Original price: P<sub>e</sub>
  - New Consumer price: P<sub>c</sub> > P<sub>e</sub>
  - New Producer price: P<sub>p</sub> < P<sub>e</sub>
  - P<sub>c</sub> - P<sub>p</sub> = Tax
### Changes in Consumer Surplus:
- P<sub>e</sub> --> P<sub>c</sub>: Lower Consumer surplus (smaller triangle)
### Changes in Producer surplus/revenue:
- TR<sub>1</sub> = P<sub>e</sub> * Q<sub>e</sub>
- TR<sub>2</sub> = P<sub>p</sub> * Q<sub>1</sub>
- TR<sub>1</sub> < TR<sub>2</sub>
- P<sub>e</sub> --> P<sub>p</sub>: Lower Producer surplus
### Government:
- Tax revenue: Q<sub>1</sub>*(P<sub>c</sub> - P<sub>p</sub>)
