# Government intervention summary

## Price ceiling
### Aims
- Provide lower-income households access to necessities by setting a `maximum legal price`, below the market equilibrium price
### Consequences
- Artificial `shortage` --> Q<sub>d</sub> increases, Q<sub>p</sub> decreases
  - This shortage will not be corrected by market forces
- Introduction of non-price rationing (coupon system, regular rations)
- Higher prices for non-capped goods, as retailers attempt to compensate
- Welfare loss
### Stakeholders
- Producers `lose`:
  - Will be disincentivized from producing as much as they would without a price ceiling
  - Loss in Total Revenue
    - TR<sub>1</sub> = P<sub>e</sub> * Q<sub>e</sub>
    - TR<sub>2</sub> = P<sub>1</sub> * Q<sub>p</sub>
    - Since p<sub>1</sub> < P<sub>e</sub> and Q<sub>p</sub> < Q<sub>e</sub> --> TR<sub>1</sub> < TR<sub>2</sub>
- Consumers `gain/lose`:
  - More low income consumers will be able to access the product, but due to the artificial `shortage`, not all of them will be able to purchase it (Q<sub>p</sub> < Q<sub>d</sub>)
- Workers `lose`:
  - As producers are disincentivized from production, they will likely `downsize` leading to higher unemployment among workers

## Price floor
### Aims
- Protect a given industry
### Consequences
- Artificial `surplus` --> Q<sub>p</sub> > Q<sub>e</sub> --> Overproduction
  - Government must compensate for the surplus (buying extra stock, exporting product, etc.)
- Promotes inefficient production
