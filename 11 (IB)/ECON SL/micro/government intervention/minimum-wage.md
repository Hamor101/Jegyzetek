# <span style="color:#fabd2f">Minimum wage
- Aims:
  - Protect workers' `standard of living`
  - Protect low-wage unskilled workers

## <span style="color:#fabd2f">1992 Princeton Study by Card & Krueger
- Looked at two states: New Jersey, Pennsylvania
- Considered the `fast food industry` for study
- Found mixed results regarding unemployment & min. wage

### <span style="color:#fabd2f">Results:
|x|Minimum Wage|Employment|
|--|--|--|
|New Jersey|Increased|Increased|
|Pennsylvania|Remained the same|Remained the same|

- Surprising result --> The opposite of what was expected
### <span style="color:#fabd2f">Why?
- Fast-food firms in the two states have monopsony[^1]
- Workers were paid below market equilibrium price for labour
- Minimum wage --> Price of labour comes closer to market equilibrium price
- The extra cost to the firms is small enough that they don't need redundancies, but workers are paid more

### <span style="color:#fabd2f">Conclusion
- Minimum wage doesn't always cause unemployment --> Effect depends on `market structure`
- A minimum wage is a `good idea` in a `monopsony`
  - `Protect` workers' quality of life
  - `Motivate` people to seek work
- A minimum wage is a `bad idea` in a `competitive market`
  - `Decrease in demand` for labour --> Unemployment
  - `Increase in price` of products --> To offload the extra labour cost from the firm to the customer --> Inflation

[^1]: A situation in which there is only one buyer for many sellers (in this case only one employer for many workers)
