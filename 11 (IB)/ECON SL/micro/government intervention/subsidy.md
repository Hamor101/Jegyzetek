# <span style="color:#fabd2f">Subsidy

> Financial support from government to firms, per unit of product

- Basically the exact `opposite of indirect tax`

- Forms:
  - Direct cash payments --> Most common
  - Other forms (low-interest loan, interest-free loan, etc.)

### Goals:
- To lower the price of goods
- Guarantee supply of necessary products
- Protect home industry

### Effects:
- Encourages production by `lowering production costs`
- Supply curve `shifts to the right`[^1]
- Lower `production cost` --> Higher `supply` --> Lower `equilibrium price`

[^1]: Distance between two supply curves vertically or horizontally: per-unit subsidy price

## Effects on stakeholders
### Consumers --> `Gain`
- P<sub>1</sub> < P<sub>e</sub> --> Lower price
- Q<sub>1</sub> > P<sub>e</sub> --> Higher quantity
### Producers --> `Gain`
-  P<sub>1</sub> > P<sub>e</sub> --> Higher revenue
### Workers --> `Gain`
- Since the producers gain --> Workers also gain
### Government --> `Lose`
- Costly for gov. expenditure
- Amount of subsidy: Q<sub>1</sub> * (P<sub>p</sub> - P<sub>c</sub>)

### Taxpayer --> `Gain`/`Lose`
- `Gain`: Taxpayers are also consumers, they will be able to buy the product at a lower price
- `Lose`: Subsidies are funded by taxes, paid by the taxpayer

## Welfare loss
- The difference between government spending and the gain in CS/PS
- WL = Q<sub>1</sub> * ΔP - (ΔCS + ΔPS)

## Total revenue
- Firms' `total revenue increases`
- TR<sub>1</sub> = Q<sub>e</sub> * P<sub>e</sub>
- TR<sub>2</sub> = Q<sub>1</sub> * P<sub>1</sub>
- Since:
  - Q<sub>e</sub> < Q<sub>1</sub>
  - P<sub>e</sub> < P<sub>1</sub>
- Therefore:
  - TR<sub>1</sub> < TR<sub>2</sub>

## Evaluation
- Promotes inefficient production
- Opportunity cost: Other industries which could be subsidized
- `Lower prices` for consumers (but that is achieved by using their taxes)
- Damage to `foreign producers`
