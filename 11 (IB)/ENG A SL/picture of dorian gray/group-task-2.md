## What is Sybil's significance? What does Sibyl represent to Dorian?

- She represents the first stepping stone towards Dorian losing his morals
- Dorian didn't really love, her, he loved that she could be many different people --> ("Her death has all the pathetic uselessness of martyrdom")
- Sybil did love Dorian --> (“I shall love him for ever!”)

## How do Basil, Dorian, and Henry react to Sybil's suicide? What does it tell us about their cahracters?
- Dorian understands that her death is his fault, grieves for a while, but he takes pride in that fact later --> (“So I have murdered Sibyl Vane”, "why is it that I cannot feel this tragedy as much as I want to? I don’t think I am heartless.")
- Henry envies Dorian because he thinks it is an honor for someone to kill themself for you -->("I wish that I had ever had such an experience.")
- Basil is grieving, feels sorry for the girl and her family, feels sad that her death is Dorian's fault 

## In chapter 9 how does Wilde create dramatic irony? How does Dorian manipulate Basil into not showing him the picture
- Dorian baits Basil into basically confessing his love, then he doesn't tell him his own secret about the portrait
- He also tells Basil that he will never speak to him again if he tries to look at the portrait --> (“If you try to look at it, Basil, on my word of honour I will never speak to you again as long as I live.")

## Basil blames Lord Henry for the change in Dorian's character. In your opinion, is Lord Henry to blame
- Partly Dorian's fault, as he is very vain, and extremely easy to manipulate
- Partly Henry's fault, because he recognizes how gullible Dorian is, and uses that to manipulate him
