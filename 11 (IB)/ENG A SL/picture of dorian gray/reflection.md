# Reflection about the Picture of Dorian Gray
I started reading the book expecting it to have the same effect on me as most other compulsory reading tasks had had thus far; I expected it to bore me to no end. I was pleasantly surprised that this was not the case. It definitely helps the book that the starting chapters and scenes work well to captivate the reader's attention and keep them interested, wanting to read more. Many previous compulsory books have failed for me in this aspect. I also found Oscar Wilde's language to be beautiful in the sense that it was enjoyable to read, irrespective of content, which is expected of a follower of the Aestheticist movement, but still pleasant to have. One weak point of the story for me was that at times it felt like the author included dialogue which was pointlessly drawn out and only tangentially or not at all related to the actual events of the story. The characters to me all felt like real people, even though most of them effectively don't exist outside the scenes of the main character. They had very well deliniated opinions and worldviews and each of them felt to have agency. Overall, I enjoyed reading the book a lot, and through it I discovered a newfound love of Late-Victorian English.
