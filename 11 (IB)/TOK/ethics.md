# <span style="color:#fabd2f">Ethics of knowledge and the knower
- How do we judge/evaluate the events of our lives?
- How do we determine what is good/right/worthy of pride?
- How ethical an action is, can be determined by different methods:
  - [Consequentialism](#consequentialism)
  - [Deontology](#deontology)
  - [Conformity](#conformity)
  - [Character](#character)
- Usually the morality of an action is evaluated by combining multiple methods

## <span style="color:#fabd2f">The trolley problem and The Good Place
- A trolley is heading down track A
- On track A five people are tied down who will die if the trolley hits them
- There is a lever that diverts the trolley to track B
- On track B there is only one person tied down who will die
- Do you pull the lever?
  - If no: You let five people die by `inaction`
  - If yes: You save five people, but `actively kill` one person

## <span style="color:#fabd2f">Consequentialism
- The morality of an action can be determined by its `consequences`
- "The ends justify the means"
- One form of conequentialism is `utilitarianism`

## <span style="color:#fabd2f">Deontology
- An action is moral if it conforms to a `set of rules`
- Doesn't look at consequences, only the `action itself`
- Famous deontologist: Immanuel Kant
  - Kant's categorical imperative: "Act only according to that maxim whereby you can at the same time will that it should become a universal maxim"
    - Basically, an action is moral if it is moral for everyone in the same situation to do the same thing as you[^1]

[^1]:E.g: "Kill everyone you don't like" is immoral, because if everyone did that, nobody would be alive anymore


## <span style="color:#fabd2f">Conformity
- An action is moral if it conforms to the `social context` of the situation

## <span style="color:#fabd2f"> Character
- An action's morality can be evaulated by the character of the person performing the action
